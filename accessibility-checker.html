Free accessibility tool

Free Website Accessibility Checker

Use this guided WCAG 2.2 self-check to review common barriers on one important page, such as your homepage, product page, or checkout. For a focused color check, use the free WCAG contrast checker.

What this tool does: It helps you perform a short manual review. It does not crawl your website, produce an accessibility score, or determine WCAG conformance.

About 5 minutes

Check one page for common barriers

Open your page in another tab and try each check. Choose "Not checked" if you are unsure.

The tool does not open or send this URL. If you request a manual review, it will prefill the contact form.

Keyboard access

Use Tab and Shift+Tab. Can you reach and operate the important links, buttons, menus, and form controls without a mouse?

Visible focus

While tabbing through the page, can you clearly see which link, button, or field has keyboard focus?

Headings and structure

Do headings describe the sections that follow and form a sensible hierarchy? Can you identify the main content?

Images and text alternatives

Do informative images have useful alternative text, and are decorative images ignored by assistive technology?

Forms and errors

Does each field have a clear label? If an error occurs, does the message explain what went wrong and how to fix it?

Links and buttons

Do link and button names explain their destination or action, including when read outside the surrounding paragraph?

Zoom and small screens

At 200% browser zoom and on a narrow screen, can you read and use the page without losing content or functionality?

Color contrast

Need to check a color pair?

Use the dedicated WCAG contrast checker to compare colors, adjust a text shade, and copy the hex value.

What to do with your results

Start with the barriers you found on the page that matters most to your visitors, such as a contact form, product page, or checkout. Retest each issue after a fix, using a keyboard and assistive technology where appropriate.

A self-check is a starting point, not a compliance verdict. W3C explains that accessibility evaluation tools cannot determine conformance on their own and that knowledgeable human review is needed.

Read W3C guidance on evaluating accessibility or browse practical accessibility guides.
