The contrast ratio is calculated for the selected text and background colors. It does not check images, gradients, transparency, non-text controls, or the rest of your page. Read W3C guidance on contrast minimums.

How to use this WCAG contrast checker

1. Choose your colors

Set the text color and the color directly behind it with the picker or hex field.

2. Review the result

Check the live ratio and the AA or AAA results for normal and large text.

3. Adjust and copy

Move the shade slider to tune the text color, then copy its hex value when it fits your design and passes your target.

What do the WCAG results mean?

For WCAG AA, normal text needs a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1, while large text needs 3:1. WCAG AAA sets higher thresholds: 7:1 for normal text and 4.5:1 for large text. Large text means at least 18 point regular or 14 point bold. The right target depends on your content and requirements, so review the W3C explanation of contrast minimums.

Contrast is only one part of accessibility. The tool does not evaluate page structure, keyboard access, labels, focus, or whether colors remain readable over imagery. For a broader first pass, use the free website accessibility self-check. You can also read the color contrast accessibility guide.
