What Is a WCAG Accessibility Audit and Why Does It Matter?

A Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) audit is a systematic evaluation of your website or web application to identify barriers that prevent people with disabilities—including blind, low-vision, deaf, motor-impaired, and neurodivergent users—from navigating, reading, and completing tasks on your site.

Automated accessibility tools can flag detectable issues such as missing alternative text or low color contrast. They cannot reliably judge whether a modal traps keyboard focus, whether a screen reader announces a dynamic cart update, whether a custom dropdown works without a mouse, or whether alternative text fits the image's context.

My manual WCAG 2.2 AA audit bridges that gap. If your project relates to the ADA in the United States, Section 508 for government procurement, the European Accessibility Act (EAA / EN 301 549), AODA in Canada, or UK accessibility requirements, you get an evidence-backed technical assessment built for remediation—not a generic scanner export. The legal requirements for a specific organization should be confirmed with qualified counsel.

What the Audit Checks

Every audit combines manual assistive technology testing with code inspection across your core templates and user journeys.

Keyboard Navigation & Focus

Full Tab, Shift+Tab, Enter, Space, Escape, and Arrow-key testing across navigation menus, dropdowns, modals, sliders, tabs, accordions, and forms. Checks logical focus order, visible focus indicators (WCAG 2.4.7 & 2.4.11), skip links, and keyboard traps.

Screen Reader Usability

Testing with NVDA, JAWS, and VoiceOver to verify heading hierarchy, ARIA landmarks, descriptive link text, accessible button names, form labels, error announcements, live regions, and logical reading order.

Forms, Errors & Checkout

Reviewing programmatic labels, placeholder misuse, required field indicators, inline validation, error summaries, and focus management across contact, login, search, and ecommerce checkout flows.

Visuals, Contrast & Zoom

Checking text and UI component color contrast ratios (WCAG 1.4.3 & 1.4.11), 200% text resize, 400% viewport reflow without horizontal scrolling, and ensuring meaning is never conveyed by color alone.

Semantic HTML & ARIA

Inspecting DOM structure for proper use of native HTML elements, valid ARIA roles/states/properties, table headers and scope attributes, and meaningful alternative text for informative vs. decorative images.

WCAG Fix Priorities

Every finding is categorized by severity (Critical, Serious, Moderate, Minor) and mapped to exact WCAG 2.2 success criteria so your team tackles high-impact blockers first.

My Step-by-Step Audit Process

  1. Scope & User Journey Selection: We agree on your target standard (typically WCAG 2.2 Level AA) and select a representative sample of templates and critical flows—such as your homepage, category/service pages, product or application pages, forms, modals, and checkout.
  2. Automated Baseline + Manual Testing: I run an initial automated sweep to catch obvious syntax and contrast issues, then perform deep manual testing using keyboard-only navigation, NVDA, JAWS, VoiceOver, and browser DevTools across desktop and mobile viewports.
  3. Prioritized Findings Report: You receive a structured, plain-English audit report with screenshot evidence, steps to reproduce, affected URLs/components, user impact notes, and WCAG 2.2 criteria references.
  4. Developer-Ready Fix Guidance: Each issue includes practical HTML, CSS, JavaScript, or ARIA remediation recommendations written so your developers—or my remediation service—can implement fixes without guesswork.
  5. Optional Remediation & Retesting: After fixes are applied, I retest the affected interactions and update your status report to confirm resolution.

Common WCAG Issues Found During Audits

  • Missing or unhelpful alt text: Informative product or diagram images with empty alt="", or decorative icons read aloud as raw filenames.
  • Low color contrast: Light gray body text, muted placeholder text, or low-contrast buttons failing the 4.5:1 / 3:1 WCAG thresholds.
  • Keyboard traps & missing focus rings: Outlines removed via outline: none, or popups/cookie banners that cannot be closed with Escape.
  • Unlabeled form controls: Inputs relying solely on placeholder attributes instead of programmatic <label> elements.
  • Broken heading hierarchy: Skipped heading levels (e.g., jumping from H1 to H4) or styling <div> elements to look like headings.
  • Inaccessible custom widgets: Mega menus, carousels, tabs, and accordion toggles built with clickable <div>s instead of semantic buttons with ARIA states.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a WCAG accessibility audit take?

Most small-to-midsize business website audits (5 to 15 core templates/flows) are completed within 5 to 7 business days. Larger ecommerce stores or custom web applications typically take 1 to 2 weeks depending on scope.

How many pages do you test in an audit?

Instead of running a shallow automated scan on 500 identical pages, I audit a representative sample of unique page templates and key user journeys (homepage, navigation, product/service pages, forms, cart/checkout, blog/article, and modals). Fixing template-level issues resolves barriers across hundreds of pages at once.

What is the difference between an automated scan and a manual WCAG audit?

Automated tools can identify some rule-based issues, but they cannot reliably assess context, keyboard interactions, screen reader announcements, logical reading order, or focus management. A manual audit checks those user flows and records findings for the agreed scope.

Do you also fix the issues after the audit?

Yes. You can hand my audit report to your internal development team, or hire me for hands-on code remediation and follow-up retesting.
