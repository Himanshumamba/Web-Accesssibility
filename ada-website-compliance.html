What Is ADA Website Compliance?

Title III of the Americans with Disabilities Act applies to places of public accommodation. How its requirements apply to a particular organization or website can depend on the facts and legal interpretation; this service provides technical accessibility work, not legal advice.

The ADA does not provide a web-specific technical checklist. WCAG 2.2 Level AA is commonly used as a technical benchmark for website accessibility work, but the appropriate legal requirements for your organization should be confirmed with qualified counsel.

Common ADA Website Barriers

Common website accessibility barriers include issues with forms, navigation, contrast, content, keyboard access, and focus.

Forms That Cannot Be Used

Missing <label> elements, placeholder-only fields, unclear error messages, inaccessible required indicators, and broken checkout or lead forms.

Navigation & Popup Barriers

Dropdown menus, mobile drawers, cookie banners, and promotional modals that cannot be opened, navigated, or dismissed with a keyboard or screen reader.

Content & Contrast Barriers

Missing alt text on product or informational images, skipped heading levels, low contrast text, and vague link text like "click here" or "read more."

Inaccessible PDFs & Media

Untagged PDF brochures, menus, and forms that screen readers cannot read, or videos lacking captions and transcripts.

How I Support ADA-Related Website Accessibility Work

  1. Manual WCAG 2.2 AA Audit: I test your key templates and user flows manually using keyboard-only navigation and screen readers (NVDA, JAWS, VoiceOver) to uncover real barriers.
  2. Prioritized Findings Report: You get a clear, documented list of issues ranked by severity, complete with screenshots and WCAG references.
  3. Code-Level Remediation: Rather than installing a third-party JavaScript overlay widget, I fix—or guide your developers to fix—the underlying HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and ARIA.
  4. Retesting & Documentation Trail: Once fixes are deployed, I retest the site and provide an updated verification summary showing concrete progress.

Why Automated Tools & Overlays Are Not Enough

An accessibility overlay may add a toolbar, but it does not by itself repair source-code barriers such as missing form labels, keyboard traps, or missing image alternatives. Automated scanners can identify some detectable issues; manual checks are needed to assess context and real interactions.

Addressing website accessibility barriers requires manual accessibility auditing and code-level remediation backed by human testing with assistive technology.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does ADA Title III apply to my business website?

That depends on the business, its services, and the applicable legal interpretation. I can test agreed pages against WCAG criteria and help with technical remediation, but I cannot determine your legal obligations. Consult qualified legal counsel for legal advice.

Can you help if I received an accessibility demand letter or audit notice?

I can review the cited pages and key user journeys, document technical findings, and help implement code-level fixes. I do not provide legal advice or prepare legal responses; consult your attorney about legal deadlines and strategy.

How long does ADA website remediation take?

A focused audit takes 5–7 business days, and common small-business remediation sprints (fixing navigation, forms, contrast, headings, and alt text) typically take 1–2 weeks depending on the platform and number of templates.
