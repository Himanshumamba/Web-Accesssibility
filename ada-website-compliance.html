What Is ADA Website Compliance?

Under Title III of the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA), places of public accommodation—which courts and the U.S. Department of Justice (DOJ) consistently interpret to include business websites, ecommerce stores, healthcare portals, and service sites—must provide equal access to people with disabilities.

While the ADA itself does not publish a line-by-line coding specification, the DOJ and federal courts routinely reference WCAG 2.1 / 2.2 Level AA as the technical benchmark for digital accessibility. When a website blocks keyboard users, screen reader users, or visitors with low vision from reading content, filling out forms, or completing purchases, it creates both a poor user experience and avoidable legal exposure.

Common ADA Website Barriers

Most demand letters and user complaints stem from recurring, preventable code and content issues.

Forms That Cannot Be Used

Missing <label> elements, placeholder-only fields, unclear error messages, inaccessible required indicators, and broken checkout or lead forms.

Navigation & Popup Barriers

Dropdown menus, mobile drawers, cookie banners, and promotional modals that cannot be opened, navigated, or dismissed with a keyboard or screen reader.

Content & Contrast Barriers

Missing alt text on product or informational images, skipped heading levels, low contrast text, and vague link text like "click here" or "read more."

Inaccessible PDFs & Media

Untagged PDF brochures, menus, and forms that screen readers cannot read, or videos lacking captions and transcripts.

How I Help Businesses Achieve Practical ADA Support

  1. Manual WCAG 2.2 AA Audit: I test your key templates and user flows manually using keyboard-only navigation and screen readers (NVDA, JAWS, VoiceOver) to uncover real barriers.
  2. Prioritized Findings Report: You get a clear, documented list of issues ranked by severity, complete with screenshots and WCAG references.
  3. Code-Level Remediation: Rather than installing a third-party JavaScript overlay widget, I fix—or guide your developers to fix—the underlying HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and ARIA.
  4. Retesting & Documentation Trail: Once fixes are deployed, I retest the site and provide an updated verification summary showing concrete progress.

Why Automated Tools & Overlays Are Not Enough

Many small businesses install an "accessibility overlay" widget hoping for one-click ADA compliance. In practice, overlays do not fix broken DOM structure, missing form labels, keyboard traps in checkout flows, or missing alt text in your source code—and automated scanners only detect 30% to 50% of WCAG failures.

Real compliance posture requires manual accessibility auditing and code-level remediation backed by human testing with assistive technology.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is my small business website required to be ADA compliant?

Yes. Most businesses serving the public in the United States fall under ADA Title III. Ecommerce stores, clinics, restaurants, service providers, and SaaS platforms are all common targets for accessibility demand letters.

Can you help if I received an accessibility demand letter or audit notice?

Yes. I can perform a rapid manual audit of the cited pages and critical user journeys, deliver a documented remediation plan, and help implement code-level fixes quickly so you can demonstrate good-faith remediation.

How long does ADA website remediation take?

A focused audit takes 5–7 business days, and common small-business remediation sprints (fixing navigation, forms, contrast, headings, and alt text) typically take 1–2 weeks depending on the platform and number of templates.
